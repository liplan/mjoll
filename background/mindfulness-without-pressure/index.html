Mindfulness without pressure to perform: why Mjoll doesn’t count streaks

Does a meditation break have to be counted in order to count?

Mjoll doesn’t count streaks and doesn’t track how often you practice. Behind this is an attitude: mindfulness doesn’t need an unbroken run of success. It is a deliberate design decision, not scientific proof that counters are bad in general.

When a break turns into a streak

A streak shows how often you have done something on consecutive days. Streaks like this can be motivating. Maybe you like the visible sign that something has found a place in your daily life.

But a different question can come up too: Am I opening the app right now for a break, or so the streak doesn’t break? This question doesn’t look down on any kind of motivation. It simply makes visible that the way a practice is displayed can become a goal of its own.

Mjoll leaves streaks out so as not to give this extra task any room. A skipped day then needs no explanation to a counter. Whether people actually experience this as less pressure would have to be studied separately.

What does the research say?

In seven studies, Silverman and Barasch examined how displayed streaks of behavior influence later decisions. Intact and broken streaks had different effects on people’s willingness to continue an activity. The way it was displayed alone could make the difference, even with the same previous behavior. The studies covered areas such as exercise and learning.1

That points to the importance of design decisions. It is not evidence that streaks make meditation worse or cause psychological harm. Nor does it prove that an app without game mechanics enables a better practice.

Regularity without obligation

You can like a familiar time of day and still not want to keep a streak. Repetition and performance tracking are different things. A break can recur without becoming part of a personal statistic.

Whether you need a break today cannot be read from a row of numbers. The frame can be reliable and still leave you the choice not to use it on a given day.

The thought behind Mjoll stays simple: you can start a session and end it again. It doesn’t have to improve any number. Maybe that is exactly the kind of freedom you are looking for.

Privacy is a separate question

A missing counter says nothing about what data an app processes. An app could show no statistics and still transmit usage data. On the other hand, settings can be stored locally without that turning into surveillance.

On your iPhone, Mjoll works without accounts, analytics, ads, tracking or a server connection. Your settings stay on the device, and so does your confirmation of the safety notes. The details are in the privacy policy.

The app and the website need to be kept apart. When you visit this website, the hosting provider GitHub processes technical data such as your IP address; purchases are handled by Apple. A setting stored locally is also something different from transmitted usage statistics. These distinctions make privacy easier to understand than a blanket “no data at all.”
