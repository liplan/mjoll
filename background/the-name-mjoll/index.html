Where does the name Mjoll come from?

What kind of snow is meant when an app is called Mjoll?

Mjöll is an Old Norse word. It means fresh, powdery snow that falls without wind and settles. The word is still used in Icelandic today. The app's name is written without the ö.

A word for a particular kind of snow

Old Norse had several words for snow. Mjöll was one of them, and it did not mean just any snow. The great Icelandic-English dictionary by Cleasby and Vigfússon translates it as “fresh powdery snow”.3

Other words grew out of it. Mjallhvítr means as white as freshly fallen snow.3 In modern Icelandic, Snow White is called Mjallhvít. Mjöll is also a woman's first name in Iceland.

Whether the word is related to mjöl, the word for flour, is not certain. The dictionary considers it possible, nothing more.3

The daughter of the snow king

Flateyjarbók, an Icelandic manuscript compiled in the late 14th century, contains a short account of how Norway was settled. It begins with a family tree. In it appears a king called Snær, which simply means snow. His children are called Þorri, Fönn, Drífa and Mjöll.1

All four names belong to winter. Fönn is lying, drifted snow, Drífa is driving snow, Mjöll is fresh powder. Þorri is still the name of a winter month in the old Icelandic calendar. The family is not a record of real people, but an image of the northern winter.

A queen after whom the snow is named

Bárðar saga Snæfellsáss tells more about Mjöll. In it, King Dumbr, who rules the sea in the far north, brings Mjöll, the daughter of Snær the Old, from Kvenland and marries her. The saga calls her the most beautiful of women and one of the tallest among humans.2

Their son is called Bárðr. He is the hero of the saga and later becomes the guardian spirit of Snæfellsjökull, the glacier in the west of Iceland. About his mother, the saga says one sentence that matters here: she was so beautiful and so fair that the snow was named after her, the snow that is whitest, falls in still air and is called mjöll.2

The saga turns the origin around. It is not the queen who bears the name of the snow, but the snow that bears the name of the queen. This is not meant as linguistic history. It is a way of telling where a word comes from.

In Orkneyinga saga, which starts from the same family tree, Mjöll does not appear. Only Snær and his son Þorri are named there.4

Anyone searching for Mjoll often comes across a character from the video game The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim. Mjoll the Lioness is a warrior in the city of Riften who can join the player's character as a companion.5 She has nothing to do with the app. Her name only sounds like the same old word.

What the name means for the app

Snow that falls in still air and muffles the sounds around you fits what the app is about. Mjoll accompanies meditation without sound, only with vibrations you can feel. Whether that reminds you of fresh snow is up to you. The name does not promise any effect.
