How do you meditate with an iPhone without following a voice or looking at the screen?

Mjoll accompanies meditation with gentle vibration patterns. You choose a program, rest your iPhone on your body and feel. This guide shows how that works step by step. We do not promise any particular feeling or result.

Before you start, please read the safety notes. The app also shows them to you before your first session.

Choosing a program

In the app, you choose a program and set the duration. Heartbeat is a designed double beat, Wave a longer wave, and there are also Slow Pulse, Deep Calm and Drift. The name Heartbeat does not mean that Mjoll measures your heartbeat or adapts to it.

Herzruhe is a guided program with a fixed ten-minute sequence. Under Custom, you put together a pattern of your own: Single, Double or Wave, plus Tempo, Intensity, Sharpness and Body.

Where to place your iPhone

You usually feel the vibration most clearly when your iPhone rests on a bone. That is why we recommend the upper breastbone, just below your neck. This works sitting or lying down, with the display facing up or down. Your iPhone should rest securely and not press.

You can also hold it loosely in your open hand, for example with your forearm supported. Which position feels better is up to your own sense of it. Whether one of the two works better has not been studied.

During the session

Keep Mjoll open. iOS only plays haptics while the app is in the foreground. That is why the screen stays on, but it is dimmed to almost black. If you lock your iPhone or switch apps, the session pauses. Open Mjoll again and tap Resume, and it continues from the same point.

Touch the screen to show the time and controls. With the slider, you set the speed between 50 and 150 percent. Mjoll remembers the speed for each program. Herzruhe has a fixed rhythm and therefore no slider, but it has a Pause button instead.

To end the session, press and hold Stop. That way nothing ends by accident while your iPhone is resting on your body. A short vibration confirms the end. When the time runs out, the vibration fades out gently.

What does the research say?

Tactile meditation and breathing devices are a research field of their own, with very different devices.1 A recent study combined vibration on the breastbone with mindfulness meditation. It examined a special device in adults with experience of trauma, so in a clinical context.2

Neither says anything about Mjoll, and neither shows that the breastbone is the better position. There is no study on Mjoll itself so far.

Your first impression counts

To begin with, one simple question is enough: Is this contact pleasant and understandable for you? If so, feel free to read on about what an anchor you can feel can be and why a break doesn’t need pressure to perform. If not, it doesn’t have to become an exercise.
